## 1. Can you describe your experience as a Product Designer working directly with Product Managers and Engineers on complex digital products? What types of products or workflows have you designed?

At [Lumanu](https://amirbeck.com/lumanu) I lead product design, code much of the app UI myself, and work with the exec team on what we build, then lead design on tax, vendor compliance, and payments from inside product and engineering. I designed a [tax engine](https://amirbeck.com/lumanu#tax-engine) to automate taxes, [Projects](https://amirbeck.com/lumanu#projects) for real time visibility and control over marketing spend, [vendor onboarding](https://amirbeck.com/lumanu#vendor-onboarding-and-compliance) across multiple redesigns using Heap funnel analytics and CS ticket data, and [reporting](https://amirbeck.com/lumanu#reporting-feature) so clients can audit their finances. Before that, at [SkySlope](https://amirbeck.com/skyslope), I improved the UX across [DigiSign](https://amirbeck.com/skyslope#digisign), [Forms](https://amirbeck.com/skyslope#forms), and [Breeze](https://amirbeck.com/skyslope#breeze), and led Breeze from concept through launch. On [EasyPeady](https://amirbeck.com/easypeady#flows) I co-wrote a PRD with the PM, then ran a brainstorm with product and engineering where developers scored each solution's build complexity 1 to 5 while designers scored its UX, so we chose the flows that were cheapest to build with the best UX.

## 2. Tell us about your experience working with established design systems in Figma. How have you used components, tokens, auto-layout, and existing patterns while also identifying when new patterns were needed?

At Lumanu [the design system and the codebase had drifted apart](https://amirbeck.com/lumanu#design-system-and-ai-tooling), so I aligned them in component UI, naming, and functionality, then used Figma Code Connect for a 1 to 1 match, and now developers pick the right component easily, mockups match development 1 to 1, and AI IDEs and code assistants work more accurately. Day to day I build from what is already there, reusable components and variants, tokens so a change moves through the system instead of being repainted screen by screen, and auto layout so a component behaves like the flex and grid it will become in code. At [Granate](https://amirbeck.com/granate#design-system) I built one from nothing, rebuilding every component under the new brand identity with dedicated Figma pages per component type for easy internal discovery and access. I reach for the existing pattern first and the bar for a new one is evidence, so when our [DigiSign](https://amirbeck.com/skyslope#digisign) tool kept the signature block selected after placement, a deviation from most other e-signing tools, and the survey on changing it split evenly because many long time users were used to it, I built a preferences page to choose the selection behavior plus a clearer flow for changing signers, and we saw a 50% reduction in digital signing mistakes.

## 3. Can you share an example of a project where you worked closely with engineers from design handoff through development? How did you manage technical tradeoffs and ensure the final shipped experience matched the intended design?

On [EasyPeady](https://amirbeck.com/easypeady) we had a PM, a UX researcher, me, and six developers, and given the nature of the timeliness of the issue we needed an MVP by the end of Q1 2021, so every tradeoff got settled with engineering rather than handed to them. [The PM and I settled on splitting the completed envelope in the backend](https://amirbeck.com/easypeady#flows) because it removed the PDF split agents were doing manually, with a fallback we had to support just in case, and we picked between the other options in a brainstorm where developers scored build complexity 1 to 5 while designers scored UX. When [SkySlope shifted strategy mid-development](https://amirbeck.com/easypeady#the-pivot) from a paid feature inside Forms to a free standalone app, I redesigned the key screens and created a basic style guide so development could quickly spin up without blockers, then designed the UI alongside development, iterating as we built and clearing UX blockers via drop-in calls while trying to get ahead. The way I hold fidelity now is to remove the handoff altogether, which is why at Lumanu I coded [~90% of the app redesign](https://amirbeck.com/lumanu#app-redesign) myself, with help on the left nav and complex layout files.

## 4. What experience do you have using AI-assisted design or development tools such as Claude and Cursor in your product design workflow? Please share a specific example of how you've used them alongside Figma.

I build and prototype with AI native, design first tools daily, including Cursor, Claude Code, Claude, Figma Make, Figma MCP, and Code Connect, and they are only as good as the system you point them at. User feedback and CS tickets pointed to a missing [reporting feature](https://amirbeck.com/lumanu#reporting-feature) at Lumanu, so I designed it in Figma on our existing system and built and launched it myself, and Figma Code Connect, the Figma MCP, and Claude Code took me from design to shipped code. That only worked because I had already [aligned the design system with the codebase](https://amirbeck.com/lumanu#design-system-and-ai-tooling), so the MCP reads the actual frame and what comes back uses our components and our tokens instead of a lookalike built out of divs, which is what makes most AI generated UI expensive to clean up. I still review the output as a designer, for token misuse, spacing that drifted, and states nobody thought about, because the tools take the translation work out of design to code but they do not decide what is worth building.
